1 — The Ogre R

      
Scottish R is a tongue-tip tap [ɾ] — one flick against the ridge behind your teeth — or a
      full trill [r] when angry. Every written R gets pronounced.

      
Say it: "buh-t'r" with one flick → then full trill: "og-rrrres." If the tongue tip
      isn't vibrating, it's not Shrek.

    

    

      
2 — Oot and Aboot

      
The "ow" glide flattens to one pure vowel: out → "oot" [ut], down → "doon", about → "aboot".

      
Say it: "Get oot. Sit doon. Whit's that aboot?" One vowel per word — if your jaw moves
      mid-vowel, you're gliding.

    

    

      
3 — DON-keh

      
Final -y drops from "ee" to "eh": happy → "happeh", donkey → "DON-keh".

      
Say it: "Happeh. Citeh. Donkeh." Then the full summons: "DON-keh!" Commitment mandatory.

    

    

      
4 — Pure vowels

      
"No" and "face" are single long vowels [noː], [feːs] — no glide at the end. Scottish
      vowels slam the door.

      
Say it: "No." Freeze your jaw the whole way through. Then "face", "hame", "stane".

    

    

      
5 — Every R gets paid

      
Unlike London English, Scottish never drops an R: father, water, ogre all end with an audible R.

      
Say it: "Watterrr. Fatherrr. Forrr everrr." Tap every single one.
